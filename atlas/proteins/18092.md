# Human eukaryotic release factor 1 (eRF1

RelaxDB entry `18092` · set RelaxDB-133 · Human eukaryotic release factor 1 (eRF1, UniProt P62495) N-terminal domain, native 1-142 plus LE (entry 1-144); NMR sample carries a C-terminal His6. The card's UniProt P03355 is the Moloney murine leukemia virus Gag-Pol polyprotein and does not match the sequence or the evidence.

## Answer

**Yes: a minor state is measured in the free protein.**

**Strict two-peak reading:** qualifies. The source shows duplicate peak sets for several residues in the free domain, intensity ratio about 5:1 (Supporting Fig. S1, Fig. 2B), which it reads as two conformational states; it states no exchange regime or rate.

In one sample of the free domain, several residues give a second resolved peak set at 10-20% (major 80-90%), so a minor conformer exists that exchanges slowly on the chemical-shift timescale, if it exchanges at all; the authors place the largest shift differences at the interface of the beta-sheet core with helices alpha2 and alpha3 and propose a reorientation of those helices (R18092-J4). Single-field model-free Rex at I45, D54-G57, K63, N67 and E134-A135 points to millisecond motion in the same region, but the source does not show it is the same process (R18092-J3).

## The free protein (reference state)

Free isolated N-domain as deposited (entry sequence, His6-tagged in the sample), no mRNA, ribosome or other eRF1 domains; 298 K, pH 6.8, 10 mM sodium phosphate, 25 mM NaCl, about 2 mM beta-mercaptoethanol, about 1 mM protein (BMRB 18092 is this paper's deposition).

## Caveats

One paper, one sample. No exchange cross-peaks, rate or full doubled-residue list is reported, and causes other than a conformational switch (proline isomerization, sample heterogeneity) were not tested, so the call rests on the authors' two-state reading of the doubled peaks. The sample is His6-tagged and in phosphate buffer, which this mRNA-binding domain might sense; no control exists. The 2LLX ensemble describes the major conformer only. Method searches used the card's viral identity, so other human eRF1 studies may exist.

## Open questions

- Not a conflict but an open link: the Rex residues (J3) and the doubled-peak conformer (J4) both sit around helices alpha2 and alpha3, but the source does not show that they are one process (r1, r2).
- The card's UniProt P03355 is the Moloney murine leukemia virus Gag-Pol polyprotein, not this sequence; the entry sequence is human eRF1 N-domain 1-142 plus LE (P62495), and the 2012 paper deposits its R1/R2/NOE under BMRB 18092. Should the entry be relabelled and the method searches rerun under human eRF1 names?
- Should the 5:1 peak doubling count as an alternate apo state when no exchange cross-peaks or rate are reported and causes such as proline isomerization or sample heterogeneity were not tested?
