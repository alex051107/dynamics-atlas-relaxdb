# Rat thyroid transcription factor 1 (Nkx2-1) homeodomain

RelaxDB entry `15521` · set RelaxDB-133 · Rat thyroid transcription factor 1 (Nkx2-1) homeodomain (UniProt P23441), 68 residues (1FTT:A; entry = author + 1)

## Answer

**Possible: exchange is detected in the free protein, but no population is measured.**

**Strict two-peak reading:** cannot tell. The source states no exchange regime, prints no kex, population or Delta-omega and shows no second peak set; its phrase 'slow conformational exchange' means motion slower than the overall tumbling of 10.5 ns, inferred from one fitted Rex at Tyr25 and from excess J(0), not from an NMR slow-exchange regime (R15521-J1, J2; E-r2). Without kex and Delta-omega the E-033 computation cannot be made.

Reference state: in the RelaxDB source sample (free homeodomain, 286 K, pH 4.3, 11.7 T) Tyr25 (entry 26) carries R2 26.39 s-1 where every other residue lies between 8.41 and 16.53 s-1, and its J(0) stands far above the rest; it is the only residue to which both the model-free and the spectral-density analysis assign an exchange contribution (R15521-J1, E-r2). The spectral-density analysis alone adds Lys21, Gln22, Ile38 and His52 (entry 22, 23, 39, 53) at the ends of helices I, II and III, which the authors read as fraying transitions and ascribe to the model-free miss being caused by an overestimated tau_m (R15521-J2). Under the single-field Rex rule of 2026-10-20 this is exchange detected in the reference state without a population, so the call is 'possible'. Context from the same paper: tau_m 10.51 ns (MF) or about 9.85 ns (RSDM); Arg1-Leu7, Arg58-Gln59 and Ala60-Gln67 move far faster than the core, Arg53 is intermediate and looks like a hinge, and the helices Gln10-Gln22, Ala28-Ile38 and Thr43-Gln50 stay ordered (R15521-J3); the paper's own 10 ns MD reproduces this and is too short for the slower process (R15521-J9). The 1H NMR structure and hydrogen-exchange abstracts put a kink or tightening at Asn51-Arg53 in the same helix (R15521-J4, J5).

## Caveats

Call raised from unknown to possible on 2026-10-20 after the RelaxDB source paper (doi:10.1111/j.1742-4658.2007.06208.x) was read in full: exchange is detected in the reference state as a single-field model-free Rex at Tyr25 plus four RSDM-only sites, and under the single-field Rex rule decided that night (ledger/redraft_notes.json, _RULE_single_field_rex) author-reported Rex in the reference state counts as exchange detected without a population. Fixed caveat: single-field Rex can also come from anisotropic tumbling; a dispersion experiment would confirm. Four of the five sites rest on the spectral-density analysis alone, which is a different interpretation of the same three measurements, not independent replication, and the authors themselves propose aromatic side-chain motion rather than backbone exchange at Tyr25. The sample has no buffer salt and sits at pH 4.3 and 286 K, conditions far from the physiological state and from the proteolysis and calorimetry rows; the domain unfolds at 32 °C without salt. Glu17, Leu34 and Lys46 were excluded for overlap, and the authors question their own local correlation times. The abstract-only row R15521-r1 is superseded and no longer cited; everything else on the rat domain is still an abstract. Built from cases S6_r6_15521 (earlier screen) and S6_r6_15521_20261020220843, with the PDF adjudication in ledger/adjudication_pdf_batch2.json.

## Open questions

- C-terminal region: mobile by NMR at pH 4.3 and 286 K (R15521-J3) versus protease-inaccessible at neutral pH (J6, r9); the proteolysis abstract names the pH difference and the methods measure different things, so this is kept as a condition difference.
- The deposited R2 of Y26 (26.3 s-1) comes with NOE 0.53, and the paper's p. 438 prints the Tyr25 exchange-related quantity as '14.67 +/- 2.35 ns', a unit that cannot be an exchange rate: should the atlas treat Y25 as exchange, as the authors do, or as a mixed fast-motion and exchange site?
