# Human NCK2 third SH3 domain (SH3-3

RelaxDB entry `7036` · set RelaxDB-133 · Human NCK2 third SH3 domain (SH3-3, UniProt O43639 198-256; entry 1-59 = UniProt - 197)

## Answer

**Unknown: not measured, or the key paper is not readable in full.**

**Strict two-peak reading:** does not. The source states that only one set of HSQC peaks is detected, in contrast to the drkN SH3 domain it cites, and concludes that the conformational exchange of SH3-3 is faster than the NMR chemical-shift timescale (R7036-J2; E-r3). No kex and no Delta-omega are printed, so no regime can be computed, and a single peak set does not by itself exclude a weakly populated or very broad second state.

No author-reported Rex exists for free SH3-3. In the BMRB 7036 source sample (free SH3-3, 20 °C, pH 6.8, phosphate with DTT, 800 MHz) the average T2 is about 80 ms against about 100 ms for the SH3-2 domain measured in the same study under the same conditions, and the HSQC peaks are visibly broader; the authors write that the domain might undergo conformational exchange on the microsecond-to-millisecond timescale and/or dynamic aggregation, keep both readings, and say that deciding needs relaxation data at several fields (R7036-J2, J12; E-r1, E-r3). The model-free analysis failed, returning order parameters no higher than about 0.3 with large exchange terms under isotropic, axially symmetric and fully asymmetric diffusion, results the authors call strange, so no per-residue Rex can be quoted (R7036-J1). The single-field Rex rule of 2026-10-20 acts on author-reported Rex; here the only exchange terms come from a fit the authors reject, and the authors' own alternative is aggregation rather than anisotropic tumbling, so the rule is not applied and the call stays unknown. The shortened T2, the single peak set (read by the authors as exchange faster than the chemical-shift timescale) and the resonance loss in the linked SH3-2-SH3-3 construct (R7036-J14) are kept as a lead for a concentration series and a dispersion experiment. Fast motion is limited: heteronuclear NOE is above 0.7 at most residues despite a far-UV CD spectrum typical of an unstructured protein (R7036-J1).

## Caveats

Call stays unknown after the BMRB 7036 source paper (doi:10.1021/bi060091y) was read in full on 2026-10-20. The single-field Rex rule (ledger/redraft_notes.json, _RULE_single_field_rex) requires author-reported Rex in the reference state; this paper reports none. Its exchange terms belong to a model-free fit that returned order parameters of about 0.3 and that the authors call strange, and its exchange reading of the short average T2 is offered together with dynamic aggregation ('and/or'), with multi-field relaxation named as the way to decide. The rule's fixed caveat (anisotropic tumbling) is not the live alternative here; aggregation is (R7036-J12, artefact_suspected, contested against R7036-J2). The evidence is a domain-average T2 compared with a neighbouring domain, with no residue, rate or population, so no site could be annotated under any call. literal_two_peak is 'does not': the authors state that only one peak set is seen and read the exchange as faster than the chemical-shift timescale. The abstract-only row R7036-r1 is superseded and no longer cited; peptide-bound and other-domain states are kept out of the apo call; no deposition readback file exists. Both readers passed all six rows of case S6_r6_7036_20261020223524 with no flags. Lead: a concentration-resolved relaxation series or a dispersion experiment would move this entry.
