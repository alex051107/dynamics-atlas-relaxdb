# Salmonella typhimurium NtrC N-terminal receiver domain (124 residues)

RelaxDB entry `4762` · set RelaxDB-133 · Salmonella typhimurium NtrC N-terminal receiver domain (124 residues), wild type

## Answer

**Yes: a minor state is measured in the free protein.**

**Strict two-peak reading:** does not. The activation exchange is too fast to be suppressed by 1000 Hz CPMG and the methyl data are fitted with a fast-exchange model; the 14% active state is seen through dispersion and the D88 shift position, and no second peak set is reported.

The unphosphorylated wild-type domain switches between inactive and active conformations. 15N CPMG combined with independently measured exchange-free R2 gives the rate, and the D88 chemical-shift ruler combined with the dispersion fit puts 14% of molecules in the active state. Methyl 1H CPMG at 18 switch-interface residues gives a similar rate, but with the population fixed at 14% rather than fitted.

## Matching structures

What the papers relate to the states:

- **2MSK**: BeF3−-activated NMR ensemble at 35 °C. Compared with the apo 2MSL ensemble, helix α4 rotates and Y94 contacts to helix 3 are replaced by Q96 contacts. An activation-mimic endpoint, not a structure fitted to the 14% minor state.
- **1DC8**: Phosphorylated receiver structure, compared with inactive 1DC7: helix-4 register shift and rotation that exposes a hydrophobic surface. Read as abstract only; no rate.
- **1KRW; 1KRX**: BeF3−-activated RDC-assisted structures showing the same register shift and a correlated T82/Y101 change as the phosphorylated form. Abstract only.

## Other conditions (not the reference state)

- BeF3−-bound WT (phosphorylation mimic), same buffer, 25 °C: yes. kex about 1,950 s-1 (1,940 ± 290 in 2009; 1,950 ± 290 or ± 200 in 2014), with the active state at ≥ 99.4% (pA ≥ 0.994), so the inactive minor state is ≤ 0.6% (converted) (NTRC-J18, NTRC-r23, NTRC-r14). A second, faster process around Y101 has a rough kex of about 30,000 ± 12,000 (unit implied s-1), population not separable (NTRC-J19, NTRC-r14).
- D86N: yes. kex 16,450 ± 840 s-1 with 43% active (NTRC-J24, NTRC-r21). D86N/A89T: yes. kex 20,050 ± 1080 s-1 with 65% active, so the inactive state is the minor one at 35% (converted) (NTRC-J25, NTRC-r22).
- Hydrogen-bond mutants: possible. S85D 2,900 ± 200, S85G 3,200 ± 900, Y101F 3,300 ± 800, Q96N 3,800 ± 600, S85D/Y101F 2,800 ± 700 s-1, no population; S85N restores 16,900 ± 1,900 s-1 (NTRC-J26 to NTRC-J31, NTRC-r24 to NTRC-r29).
- Y101L: possible. kex 2,200 ± 300 s-1 at 18 listed residues (NTRC-J34, NTRC-r15); the faster Y101-linked process is absent except at S13, where sodium binding is suspected (NTRC-J35, NTRC-J36). BeF3−-bound Y101L 1,500 ± 600 s-1 (NTRC-J37, NTRC-r18).
- Phosphorylated WT: unknown. The 2001 abstract says phosphorylation shifts the equilibrium toward the active state, with no rate or residues (NTRC-J14, NTRC-r5).

## Caveats

All Kern-lab NMR samples use 50 mM sodium phosphate for a phosphorylatable domain with no phosphate-free control, and the authors suspect sodium binding at S13 (NTRC-J36); the NMR collaborator is asked whether to flag the 14% and kex as buffer-dependent. The population rests on one ruler residue (D88) combined with dispersion. The minor state is called active from its shifts; no structure was fitted to it, and the activated structures are BeF3− or phosphorylated endpoints, two of them read as abstracts. MD macrostate populations (about 52% and 48%, not assigned to active or inactive; NTRC-J6, NTRC-r1) were not used. The RelaxDB R1/R2/NOE sample (2001 study, abstract only) is unidentified.

## Open questions

- Same unphosphorylated WT state, two dispersion methods: 15N backbone kex 13,580 ± 850 s−1 (r20) versus methyl 1H kex 15,458 ± 452 s−1 (r7); the authors call them in excellent agreement, but the methyl fit fixed pB at the 15N-derived 14%, so the population is not an independent replicate.
- BeF3−-bound WT rate printed three ways: 1,940 ± 290 s−1 (2009 Fig. 2D, r23), 1,950 ± 290 s−1 (2014 main text, r14) and 1,950 ± 200 s−1 (2014 Fig. 1C, r14); S85D (2,900 ± 200) and Y101F (3,300 ± 800) are identical in the 2009 and 2014 papers (r24/r12, r27/r13), so they are probably one data set each and not replicates.
- Methyl probe count: the methyl paper's narrative says 21 exchanging probes while Table 1 lists 22 methyl groups including the excluded I55-γ2 (r7, r9).
- Which sample is the RelaxDB 4762 R1/R2/NOE set: unphosphorylated, phosphorylated or the partially active mutant from the 2001 relaxation study (r4–r6, abstract only), and at what temperature and buffer? Without it the ps-ns window cannot be judged.
- All Kern-lab NMR samples used 50 mM sodium phosphate for a phosphorylatable receiver domain with no phosphate-free control, and the 2014 authors suspect sodium binding at S13; should the atlas carry the 14% active population and kex values as the WT apo answer as they stand, or flag them as buffer-dependent until a control exists?
